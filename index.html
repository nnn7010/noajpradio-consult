About Noa

I know what it feels like to study for years and still feel unable to use the language.

From junior high school through university, I struggled with English classes. I studied hard and followed the usual school advice, but I still could not speak English the way I wanted.

Later, I completed a master's degree, and my graduate research changed how I look at learning. It taught me to look beyond simple labels like “effective” or “meaningless” and ask what a method is actually training, what ability it builds, and when it becomes useful.

That perspective helped me rethink my own English learning. I did not rely on English schools, conversation schools, or online lessons to solve everything. I kept looking for methods that fit my situation. I still would not call myself perfectly fluent, but English is now part of my real life: I connect with friends overseas and communicate in English every day with my French partner.

That is the same perspective I bring to Japanese learning advising. My role is not to force one perfect method on you, but to help you understand what your study is building and create a system you can keep using.
